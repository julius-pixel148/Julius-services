Liners

When a chimney needs a new liner.

The liner is what contains the heat and smoke on the way up. An inspection tells us whether yours is doing its job.

Reason 01

Cracked clay tiles

Older chimneys are lined with clay tiles that crack with age, heat, and settling. Cracks let heat and smoke reach the brick and the framing around it.

  • Found by camera inspection
  • Common in older homes
  • Relined in stainless steel
Reason 02

A new insert or stove

An insert or stove needs a liner sized to its outlet. Venting it into a large open flue causes poor draft and fast creosote buildup.

  • Sized to the appliance
  • Better draft
  • Cleaner burn
Reason 03

No liner at all

Some older chimneys were built without a liner. Bare brick and mortar break down from heat and the acids in smoke.

  • Older chimneys
  • Protects the masonry
  • Brings the flue up to standard

FAQ

Cap & liner questions.

Anything else, ask us when you book.

01Do I really need a chimney cap?

Yes. A cap keeps rain from running straight down the flue, keeps birds, squirrels, and raccoons from moving in, and stops leaves and needles from blocking it. It's one of the least expensive ways to protect a chimney.

02There are birds or animals in my chimney. What can you do?

We can install a cap with mesh to keep animals out. Some birds are protected by federal law while they're nesting, so timing matters. We'll tell you what can be done and when.

03What is a chase cover?

Factory-built fireplaces often vent through a wood-framed box called a chase, with a metal cover on top. The original covers are usually thin steel that rusts through, which lets water into the chase. A sloped stainless steel cover fixes it.

04What does a top-sealing damper do?

It closes the flue at the top with a rubber gasket, which seals much tighter than an old cast-iron damper. That keeps out rain and animals, and keeps heated air from escaping up the chimney when the fireplace isn't in use.
